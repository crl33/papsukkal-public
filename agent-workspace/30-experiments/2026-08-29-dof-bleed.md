# Foreground bleeding onto the midground

## Why

Reported: "the foreground focal point bloom bleeds into the midground stems and
grass." Sharp stems near a big defocused foreground mass were being washed with
its colour.

## What was changed

Architectural, not a parameter. The composite did:

```
w = max(farCoC, nearCoC, nearSpread)   // nearSpread = blurred near coverage
```

so a *sharp* midground pixel adopted the whole blur pyramid whenever a blurred
foreground was anywhere nearby — pulling in foreground colour.

Rebuilt the near field as a **premultiplied scatter layer**: prefilter writes
`color × nearCoC` and `nearCoC`, the pyramid blurs both together, and the
composite un-premultiplies (`rgb / a`) and composites *over* the far-field
result with a saturated coverage ramp.

## Numbers

Measured near-layer alpha before the fix: 0.66 inside the foreground mass
(should be ~1.0) but 0.29 on midground stems and 0.17 on the hero — coverage was
spreading far wider than the true circle of confusion.

## Result

Kept. Zero coverage now means the midground is left exactly as it was: a
defocused foreground can occlude, but it can no longer tint.

## What the next agent should know

Two follow-on subtleties, both needed:

1. Un-premultiply before compositing, or edges darken.
2. A near-field pixel must also lose **its own** sharpness — gather DOF still
   holds the foreground object in the base layer, so blur the base by the
   pixel's own near CoC. Without this a small near flower shows crisp petals
   through a faint smear.
